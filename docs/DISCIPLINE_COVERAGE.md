# Discipline Coverage

The monitor uses multi-label categories: a spacecraft avionics role can appear in Aerospace and Hardware/Electrical, while a structures role can appear in Aerospace and Mechanical. This improves field-specific discovery without duplicating the underlying role or its notification identity.

## Maintained title vocabulary

- Mechanical: mechanical, electromechanical, mechatronics, thermal/fluids, heat transfer, product design/development, mechanisms, machine design, vehicle dynamics, powertrain/chassis, hydraulics/pneumatics, HVAC/refrigeration, rotating equipment, turbomachinery, combustion, acoustics/vibration, FEA/CFD/CAE, design/test/validation, equipment, tooling, materials, structures, stress, and loads/dynamics.
- Aerospace: aerospace, aeronautical, avionics, propulsion, GNC, flight systems/sciences/controls/test/dynamics/mechanics/software, aerodynamics/aerothermal, aeroelasticity, aerostructures, airframe, aircraft systems/design/integration/certification, spacecraft/satellite/launch vehicle, space systems, mission systems/design/operations/integration, astrodynamics/orbital mechanics, mass properties, survivability, rotorcraft, airworthiness/flightworthiness, payload, structures, stress, and loads/dynamics.
- Manufacturing/Industrial: manufacturing, industrial, production, process development, quality and supplier quality, tooling, materials and process, facilities, automation/controls, NPI, and continuous improvement.
- Product Management: product manager, technical product manager, associate product manager, product management, product mgmt, and APM. Product design, project management, and program management are not treated as PM by name alone.

The vocabulary is based on real employer naming. Boeing's early-career pages explicitly separate aerospace/avionics, mechanical/mechatronics, manufacturing, materials, industrial, and structural disciplines. RTX's early-career and operations programs use mechanical, aerospace, industrial, manufacturing, quality, mechatronics, aeronautical/astronautical, process-manufacturing, and production-support titles. Johnson & Johnson posts Product Management Intern and Product Management Co-op titles. These sources are useful regression references:

- [Boeing engineering internships](https://jobs.boeing.com/internships)

## Employer expansion

The company registry includes dedicated aerospace, automotive/EV, industrial automation, HVAC, semiconductor-equipment, medical-device, energy, engineering-services, and materials/manufacturing cohorts in addition to technology companies. The current registry has 430 employers; 112 declare mechanical-related role families, 46 declare aerospace-related role families, and 53 declare product-related role families.

Curated structured sources include Northrop Grumman, BAE Systems, GE Aerospace, Lockheed Martin, RTX, The Aerospace Corporation, Moog, Woodward, Varda Space, Johnson Controls, Medtronic, Nissan, and GE Vernova. The normal discovery layer continues probing official careers pages for the rest, and source success is reported separately from registry size.

## Standout designation and recommendations

The fire emoji is discipline-specific. It means a company is a curated standout for the category currently being viewed, using industry scale, technical reputation, and established early-career programs; it is not a universal company ranking. For example, Palantir is marked on Software Engineering pages, while Northrop Grumman is marked on Aerospace and Mechanical pages but not Software Engineering pages. `data/company_metadata.json` is the sole source for the designation.

Recommendation presets cover Software, AI/ML, Data, Product Management, Aerospace, Mechanical, Hardware/Electrical, and Manufacturing/Industrial employers. They are starting points only: the notification UI selects nothing by default, and users can add or remove any company before opting in.
